People like a lane. Data scientist *or* full stack. Notebook *or* production. Insights *or* interfaces.

I did not pick one. The work that pays and the work that ships both sit on this site because they are the same job from different ends: turn a mess into a thing a human can use by Friday.

The tagline is [Developer • Data Engineer • Writer](/). The title is Data Scientist & Full Stack Developer. That is not a hedge. It is a refusal to hand a client a pickle file and a shrug about “the engineering team.”

## The split that wastes money

A typical programme:

- A data person builds a model that predicts churn in a notebook.
- A slide says 87%.
- An engineering team is asked to “productionize.”
- Six weeks later the model is a cron job nobody trusts, and the UI is a CSV download.

Or the inverse:

- A web team ships a dashboard of counts.
- A stakeholder asks why.
- Nobody in the room can join to the grain of the warehouse.
- A data person is hired to “add AI.”

I have been both people. The cheaper pattern is one owner for the loop: the number, the interface, the handoff. That is the studio.

## What “data scientist” means here

Not a Kaggle medal. Not a research lab.

It means I can:

- Write the SQL that answers the meeting question, with a grain and a denominator. See [SQL that survives a standup](/articles/sql-that-survives-a-standup).
- Put a scikit-learn pipeline on [disease prediction](/projects/disease-prediction-model) with features and enough explainability that a reviewer can see the lever.
- Build the Power BI surface for [Spotify](/projects/spotify-analytics-dashboard) or an Amazon store without hiding logic in DAX.
- Tell you when a model is the wrong artefact. Sometimes the artefact is a view and a recommendation.

B.Tech Mathematics from the Federal University of Technology is the floor: statistics, data structures, the habit of defining a thing before estimating it. The internships at Mentorness/CareerEX were the first time those habits met a stakeholder who did not care about the notebook. The freelance years are the rest.

## The writer is not a brand extra

Writing is how the work travels when I am not on the call.

A runbook is writing. A metric definition is writing. A cover letter that cites the posting is writing. The articles on this site are the same muscle as the handoff folder: sentences a stranger can use.

I will not outsource that to a model and hit send. A model can draft. A human still has to know whether “net units” includes the returns that landed next week.

## How the two trades fix each other

Data without a site is a brief that never meets a user. You can hide a bad grain in a notebook. You cannot hide it in a product that a job seeker uses at 11pm.

A site without data is a costume. A career platform that does not infer skills from bullets is a form. A support bot that does not retrieve is a parrot. A storefront that cannot change price without a deploy is a brochure.

When I hold both, the shortcuts get embarrassing faster. That is the point.

Practices I stole from one side and use on the other:

- **Fixtures.** Matcher evals, RAG questions, merchandising sample products. Tests are not only for functions.
- **Grain.** A page has a grain too. One job per screen. One primary action.
- **Denominators.** A conversion tile without sessions is a vanity metric. A hero without a next click is the UI version.
- **Handoff.** A model without a runbook is the same sin as an app without a README.
